# CATS-NPU final research contribution assessment

**Decision: NO-GO for manuscript preparation on the mechanisms investigated through Phase 9. Date: 2026-10-28.** The strongest implementation is a useful exact-or-reject consumer-NPU prototype. Its order encoding, stable selection, hierarchy, and output checking do not establish a distinct research mechanism or a new guarantee over their closest established equivalents. The compiler-source audit also supplies an existing explanation consistent with the INT16/INT64 failures. More benchmark sessions cannot resolve this mechanism-equivalence problem. This conclusion is limited to the investigated approaches; it is not an impossibility theorem for future exact NPU selection.

This report closes the conditional investigation in `STATIC_NPU_NOVELTY_REASSESSMENT.md`. It preserves the original Phase 7/8 interpretations and treats all new-method Phase 9 timings as exploratory. No final confirmatory study, manuscript, or journal figure was produced. The final archive's `manifest.json` identifies the source revision, evidence hashes, and package scope.

**Contribution statement and scope.** No original Top-K algorithm or novel semantics-preserving compilation mechanism has been established. What this project establishes within its measured scope is: (1) explicit Intel NPU execution of static local/global Top-K graphs; (2) reproducible failures in particular numerical/type paths; (3) a tested INT32 order-code workaround with stable device selection and full host rank verification; and (4) a substantial development-pilot improvement over native long-vector NPU Top-K, with remaining CPU/CUDA disadvantages. These are engineering findings and correctness diagnostics. Their possible usefulness does not satisfy the user's originality gate.

The hardware evidence was collected on a Core Ultra 7 255H, Intel AI Boost 3720, Windows build 26200, OpenVINO 2026.4.1, recorded NPU driver 1004512/compiler 458778, and RTX 5060 Laptop GPU. The environment record is `results/doctor.json`; individual experiments and manifests retain their dates. The final audit performed no new inference or performance experiment.

**Novelty investigation.** On 2026-10-28 the final audit checked primary papers, official specifications, and upstream source for exact accelerator selection, integer ordering, deterministic ties, width restrictions, numerical compiler transformations, and result checking. Search terms included NPU/OpenVINO TopK precision conversion, floating-point radix keys, constrained-width deterministic selection, hierarchical/fused Top-K, and verified floating-point compilation. This is a focused comparison of the actual mechanisms, not a claim of an exhaustive systematic review or proof that no unpublished idea could exist. The decisive evidence is constructive equivalence to known components, not failure to find the CATS-NPU name in a search.

RadiK [R5] and RTop-K [R6] are relevant exact GPU selection techniques but are not claimed to be identical to the CATS graph. Their relevance is to eliminate broad claims that accelerator thresholding, resource-aware selection, or numerical keys are new. Sample-guided exact selection with complete-row certification also exists [R13]. DeepSelect's local/global path is the closest structural precedent, while CUB and the OpenVINO stable attribute are the closest direct precedents for the strongest INT32 implementation.

**Resolution of the type-semantics question.** The pinned public NPU compiler release source `c74b1265f28dc5a8f08dd50babd2a063cb59ac6e` contains these rules:

- `adjust_software_ops_precision.cpp` treats TopK inputs as legal when they are FP16, FP32, or 32-bit integers. Its `TopKConverter` inserts an input conversion to FP16 and converts the selected values back to the original type for other input types [R10].
- `convert_precision_to_i32.cpp` maps signed/unsigned 64-bit integers to their 32-bit counterparts and applies type conversion to TopK and other IE operations [R11]. The same essential rules are present in inspected development revision `88db57024ce8822a702679033ef0d0b6ea2b3cf8`.

The final CPU audit predicts the preserved physical observations exactly: all ten returned INT16 SIFT codes equal `int16(float16(original_code))`, including −7853 becoming −7852; all three reconstructed INT64 probe cases match signed-32-bit narrowing for both winner IDs and values. In the 2³²-crossing case this predicts the wrong winners `[511,510,509,508]` instead of `[3,2,1,0]`. The reconstructed original reference values also match the records. These checks are in `results/final_research/20261028/audit.json`, with unmodified pinned compiler files and their license in `upstream/`.

This is source-supported consistency, **not an installed-binary causal trace**: the exact source revision of the packaged compiler binary and its executed IR pass sequence have not been identified. The FP16-subnormal root cause also remains unisolated. The report therefore does not attribute these failures to faulty NPU silicon or claim a newly discovered universal compiler defect. It does establish that the observed INT16/INT64 patterns are already expressible by existing upstream narrowing rules; a novel hidden precision-analysis mechanism has not emerged.

**Selection contract and supported abstract domain.** Input `x` is an immutable one-dimensional array of finite, native FP16 scores. Rank pairs by score descending, then ascending original zero-based index; −0 and +0 are equal scores. NaN, infinities, other dtypes, and malformed shape/K are unsupported. For the deployed two-level stable graph, use integers `N>W>=K>=1`, `M=ceil(N/W)`, and `MW<=2^31-1`, so every padded global ID and the candidate dimension fit the INT32-based graph. The tested configurations are a very small subset of this abstract size domain; allocation and compiler support for arbitrary large shapes are not guaranteed. Compilation/runtime errors or failed checks explicitly reject execution.

The final retained implementation consists of `src/cats_npu/order_encoding.py`, the graph and checked runner in `scripts/probe_stable_integer_hierarchy.py --key-dtype i32`, and `certify_rank` in `src/cats_npu/static_graph.py`. The implementation was already a Phase 9 research prototype, rather than a new final method invented by this audit. Closeout changes add explicit shape/index-capacity guards and cancel NPU execution on a failed CPU graph preflight; they do not add a new selection mechanism. Historical baselines and failed ablations are preserved. Current scripts can reproduce the methods; historical manifests designate source snapshots, rather than a prospectively frozen implementation before every exploratory observation. That distinction prevents treating their timing as final source-frozen validation.

**Ordering proof.** Let `b` be a binary16 word and replace the word for −0 by that for +0. Define `q(b)=65535-b` for negative words and `q(b)=b xor 32768` for nonnegative words. For negative finite numbers the binary16 word order is reversed relative to numerical order; complementation reverses it back. For nonnegative finite numbers word order agrees with numerical order; flipping the sign bit preserves that order while placing it above negative codes. Zero normalization makes both zeros equal. Thus `x_i<x_j` iff `q(x_i)<q(x_j)`, and equal numerical scores have equal codes. Finite codes lie in `[1024,64511]`. This is the established radix-order transformation [R1], not a new theorem.

The final audit exhaustively sorts **all 63,488 finite binary16 bit patterns** both numerically (FP64 exactly represents them) and by `q`, using original index for ties. All 63,488 sorted positions agree, not just the largest 100. There are 63,487 distinct numeric codes because the two zeros coincide, and 2,046 nonzero subnormal patterns. This exhaustively checks the finite encoding domain; it does not enumerate all NPU input vectors, lengths, K values, or compiler states. Tests of the bounded packed representation were also strengthened to check its complete finite-word ordering rather than only its first 100 results.

**Stable hierarchy proof.** In each tile an element omitted from its exact stable local K set has at least K better pairs in that tile. It therefore cannot be in the global K set. Every global winner lies in the union of local K sets. Within a tile, stable value-sorted TopK preserves ascending local index among equal scores. In the tile-major candidate concatenation, all candidates from earlier tiles precede equal-score candidates from later tiles. Hence the candidate order among equal scores agrees with ascending global index. An exact stable final TopK over the codes therefore selects the required global K pairs. A padding code below every valid code cannot displace a real winner; even a short final tile retains all its real elements when fewer than K exist there. Gathered IDs refer to the original positions. These are conditional algorithmic facts: they assume the integer, gather, indexing, and stable TopK primitives execute their stated semantics.

**Width bounds.** The stable INT32 graph stores a 16-bit score-order code in INT32 and carries indices separately. It does **not** pack a 16-bit score and a 20-bit million-element index into one INT32 key, so that combined-width restriction does not apply to it. For the separate packed experiment, with code span `S=max(q)-min(q)+1`, its reserved-sentinel key is `((q-min(q))(N+1)+(N-1-i))-2^31+1`. The implemented sufficient capacity bound is `(S-1)(N+1)+N-1 <= 2^32-2`. Outside it the encoder rejects. This is ordinary positional encoding with a finite-width guard.

The split-key alternative uses a local key `q(W+1)+(W-1-local_index)`. For `W<=256` its maximum is 16,579,582. Let `tau` be the candidate Kth code and let `g` be 2 for codes above `tau`, 1 for equal codes, and 0 below it. Global priority `g(N+1)+(N-global_index)` separates these groups and selects the smallest IDs at the boundary. All above-threshold candidates must be retained because there are fewer than K of them. For `N<=5,592,404` its maximum `3N+2` fits within 2^24. These keys consequently fit INT32 and the exact-integer range of FP32. This does not protect them against arbitrary compiler conversions, FP16 lowering, or incorrect primitives; it is a representation bound, not a hardware proof. Supported stable TopK provides the same required order more simply on this stack.

**Exact-or-reject proof, independent of NPU selection correctness.** The checker trusts host FP16 comparisons, host integer operations, and an immutable source array. It first requires K distinct valid IDs and consistent returned source values/codes. It orders those K IDs by the required score/index rule. Let `(t,j)` be their last pair. It computes `R = count(x>t) + count(x[:j]==t)`. The K chosen pairs contain exactly K−1 pairs better than `(t,j)`. If the complete input also contains exactly K−1 such pairs (`R=K−1`), those chosen pairs exhaust the true better set; with `(t,j)` they are exactly the global stable Top-K. Otherwise it rejects. The actual score/index selection is never replaced by CPU Top-K. This establishes soundness of a successful return under the host assumptions even if NPU selection is wrong. It establishes **neither total success nor universal correctness of the NPU primitives**. Host corruption, concurrent mutation, or a faulty host comparison/checker is outside that safety argument.

The checker was additionally tested on every nonempty candidate subset for every K of vectors of lengths 1–4 over `{−minimum_subnormal,−0,+0,+minimum_subnormal}`: 4,340 candidate-subset checks. Acceptance agrees with an independent stable ordering in that bounded domain. The mathematical proof, rather than this finite test, supplies the general safety argument. This is an instance-checking construction within the established certifying-algorithm approach [R8]. In contrast, verified floating-point compilation establishes properties of the compiled semantics itself [R9]; CATS-NPU has no such compiler proof.

**Cost model boundary.** Encoding is O(N); sorting K returned indices is O(K log K); full rank verification is O(N). The graph has `M` logical local selections of width W and one selection of width MK, carried in one OpenVINO request. Its opaque device cost can be written structurally as `C_local(M,W,K)+C_global(MK,K)`, plus runtime submission, staging, and host work. This is not a validated prediction model or new asymptotic bound. Software-visible INT32 input tensors use `4MW` bytes and outputs use `8K` bytes. Those are tensor sizes, not measured physical bus traffic or peak hardware-memory measurements. A synchronous infer interval includes runtime and waiting costs, so it is not pure device-kernel latency.

**Physical correctness evidence already collected.** The native FP16 static graph rejected the subnormal-ramp case when its returned boundary ranked 64 at K=4. The encoded INT32 graph passed that case; the 63,488-word vector containing every finite FP16 representation; boundary ties; SIFT1M/BEIR development query 0; and 100 same-process adversarial cases at N=512,K=10. The latter used all-equal, coarse ties, boundary ties, signed-zero/subnormal, and random FP16 groups. Irregular/padded sizes also occur in the BEIR and edge probes. The split-key and packed alternatives passed their recorded cases; INT16/INT64 each have counterexamples. One vector containing every finite score does not exhaust arbitrary combinations or certify every code as a boundary winner. None of these facts imply zero failure probability on unseen inputs. Existing observations are in `results/phase9/` and `results/phase9_novelty/`; the final audit verified all **49 raw-file hashes** in their two manifests.

**Performance and mechanism isolation.** The decisive existing same-process pilot alternated methods on SIFT1M development query 0, N=1,000,000,K=10,W=256, with five timed repetitions per method. Input was host FP16 scores and output host stable IDs. Compilation and first inference were excluded. Source encoding, returned-output copying, and full rank checking were included for the INT32 paths; independent reference construction was outside the timing. CPU/CUDA use the corrected repository implementations and their full host-input/host-output costs.

The raw-file audit is authoritative for exact numbers: `results/phase9_novelty/paired_sift_dev_q0000_n1000000_k10_with_stable.json` and `results/final_research/20261028/audit.json`. The stable INT32/direct ratio is a descriptive **4.267×** speedup, with stable INT32 13.7% slower than plain FP16 static selection. Stable-INT32 median component observations were 5.282 ms encoding, 20.289 ms staging/infer, and 3.322 ms copying/verification; separate component medians do not sum to the total median. Median sequential throughput implied by 30.860 ms is 32.4 million scores/s, not measured concurrent throughput. These observations demonstrate an implementation tradeoff and do not establish novelty or an advantage over the simplest equivalent integer method, which is the stable INT32 path itself.

Earlier hybrid fixed/adaptive NPU methods used 489 submissions at the million-score K=10 SIFT development case; the static graphs used one. Their separate pilot medians were approximately 260–321 ms, whereas direct native NPU was 143 ms. These different pilot runs are not pooled with the interleaved comparison above. Separate stable-INT32 development query-0 probes returned correct results at 0.966 ms for SciFact N=5,183 and 3.761 ms for FiQA N=57,638; their series can include cold inference and must not be compared as controlled paired effects against earlier baselines. Wider FP16 exploration showed K and W sensitivity, but no causal kernel model was isolated.

Native stable full-vector TopK, direct INT32, all required baselines across representative new queries, and independent sessions were **not** evaluated in a final frozen experiment. That missing evaluation is recorded as an unpassed gate rather than replaced by comparisons to a weak baseline. The CPU/CUDA disadvantage is not itself a reason for NO-GO: the failure is the absence of a distinct mechanism/new guarantee and of a demonstrated advantage over the closest equivalent approach.

**Statistical decision.** No new confirmatory hypothesis/protocol was frozen after the final novelty gate failed. The pilot's five measurements are one process, not five independent sessions. No confidence interval, inferential p-value, generalization claim, or stable tail guarantee is reported for the new methods. The old Phase 7 and Phase 8 independent sessions test their original methods/protocols and are not transferred to this mechanism. A new final validation campaign would be methodologically possible, but its results would not change the demonstrated equivalence of the present mechanism to established techniques. The project therefore stops this direction now, as requested. No prior holdout is relabeled untouched and no question is changed after a negative final test.

**Final disposition.** NO-GO is firm for manuscript preparation under the requested original exact-selection mechanism scope. The earlier conditional outcome is closed by the source/novelty audit, rather than extended to an unspecified next benchmark. The retained stable INT32 prototype and numerical counterexamples can support engineering reuse and further investigation of compiler behavior. A future publication decision would require a fundamentally distinct mechanism or verified property with a clear closest comparator; a new name, additional benchmark scale, or a larger measured speedup alone would not reopen this decision.
