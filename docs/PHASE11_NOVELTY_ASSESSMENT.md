# Phase 11 originality assessment

**2026-10-29 — NO-GO at Gate A for the investigated calibration mechanisms.** This assessment concerns ranking fidelity during similarity computation, which is a new problem for CATS-NPU. It accepts the Phase 2–10 negative findings without reopening selection algorithms. The strongest concrete calibration candidate reduces to the straightforward rank-aware rotation baseline that the mission explicitly excludes as sufficient novelty. No distinct optimization capability, theoretical improvement, or hardware-specific mechanism was established. Development stops before embedding regeneration, calibration training, or physical scoring experiments.

The starting and inspected HEAD is `345c24cebd717b57e167ca6e696bbcd1e819b2d4`; no newer commit was present. This is a focused primary-source review through the current date, not a systematic-review completeness claim or a proof that no original method could ever exist. Failure to locate an identical Intel FP16 implementation is not used as evidence of originality.

**Closest work.** Sources were checked on 2026-10-29. Numbered references and source inspection details appear below and in `results/phase11/20261029/literature_review.json`.

| Work | Established capability relevant here | Material difference from this project; implication |
|---|---|---|
| SpinQuant, ICLR 2025 [R1] | Learns orthogonal transformations against quantized task loss while freezing pretrained weights; Cayley/Stiefel optimization. | LLM transformations and low-bit quantization, not standalone FP16 retrieval. Changing its task loss/placement does not establish a new optimizer or invariance property. |
| QuaRot, NeurIPS 2024 [R2] | Randomized structured rotations exploit computational invariance to facilitate low-bit computation. | Its uniform low-bit quantizers differ from FP16 floating-point rounding. Rotation benefits cannot simply be transferred numerically to this device. |
| TORQUE, September 2026 preprint [R3] | Optimizes high-precision coordinate retention before and after rotation under a bit budget; provides an error bound. | Mixed storage/quantizer design, not a demonstrated NPU scoring path. Retention plus rotation is not a new mechanism here. |
| OPQ, CVPR 2013 [R4] | Optimizes decomposition/rotation and codebooks for reconstruction distortion. | PQ is not FP16 GEMM. A reconstruction-trained rotation is a control, not the proposed invention. |
| Pairwise Quantization, 2016 [R5] | Learns a linear preprocessing transform to reduce scalar-product/distance distortion rather than point reconstruction error. | Primarily asymmetric quantization. It directly precedes the broad claim that interaction-aware embedding preprocessing is new. |
| Anisotropic Vector Quantization, ICML 2020 [R6] | Weights inner-product errors according to retrieval relevance; derives a corresponding anisotropic loss. | Different quantizer and assumptions. Rank-sensitive precision allocation is already motivated mathematically. |
| Distill-VQ, SIGIR 2022 [R7] | Uses fixed dense embeddings as teachers; studies score, margin and ranking distillation objectives, including RankNet. | Learns IVF/PQ rather than our R. This is the closest loss/teacher precedent; a rotation-constrained student remains a straightforward baseline. |
| MoPQ, EMNLP 2021 [R8] | Learns quantization using a matching objective instead of reconstruction alone. | Joint supervised retrieval training is outside the requested frozen-encoder scope. The broader ranking-versus-MSE insight is established. |
| Reliable Evaluation Protocol for Low-Precision Retrieval, ACL 2026 [R9] | Reports spurious score ties and proposes higher-precision scoring and tie-aware evaluation. | Evaluation/scoring control rather than embedding calibration. A fair new method must earn value beyond that simple precision control. |
| Undetected Damage of Quantization on Retrieval, September 2026 preprint [R10] | Links retrieval changes to margins and precision allocation. | The paper explicitly distinguishes encoder quantization from stored-vector compression; it does not establish our NPU's damage rate. |
| Teacher-guided residual calibration, KBS 2026 [R11] | Publisher material describes lightweight residual calibrators and teacher-guided rank distillation with fixed quantized galleries. | Cross-modal PQ, potentially changes geometry; full text was inaccessible. Used only for the documented broad calibration precedent, not an implementation-equivalence claim. |
| Target-embedding gradient optimization, AAAI 2026 [R12] | Directly updates cached target embeddings with similarity-based negative sampling. | Changes target geometry and belongs to quantized-index training. It does not satisfy orthogonal invariance merely by avoiding repeated corpus encoding. |
| Learned rotation-aware binary projections, SIGIR 2026 [R13] | Learns retrieval-oriented binary projections incorporating a random orthogonal transform. | Binary shortlisting and float reranking differ from FP16 full scoring; rotation combined with a retrieval objective already has concrete retrieval precedent. |
| SmoothQuant, ICML 2023 [R14] | Uses reciprocal per-channel scaling to preserve a linear map while changing quantization behavior. | INT8 uniform quantization rather than FP16. Dual scaling alone cannot be claimed as a new calibration mechanism. |

The contrast is important: these papers do not collectively prove that the exact requested FP16 method is already published. They establish the mechanisms our actual candidates would reuse. A combination could be original if it introduced a new interaction property; no such property was derived here.

**Concrete candidate and closest equivalent baseline.** Let the fixed reference scores be `s(q,d)`, and let `h_R(q,d)` denote the target low-precision scoring function after a common orthogonal transform. A straightforward baseline trains only R using calibration-query document pairs:

    minimize over R with R^T R = I:
      sum_(q,i,j) w_qij * ell(sign(s(q,di)-s(q,dj)), h_R(q,di)-h_R(q,dj)).

Here `ell` can be a logistic/hinge ranking loss or a margin-distillation loss; weights may emphasize small-margin pairs. The original encoder remains frozen; corpus preprocessing is offline and query rotation is online. This is the candidate's complete operative content. It is also the mission's stipulated simple rank-aware rotation baseline. Using a different standard orthogonal parameterization, FP16 straight-through surrogate, or device-measured loss changes implementation choices without demonstrating another scientific capability.

The apparently stronger proposal to optimize *correlated score errors* gives `e_R(q,di)-e_R(q,dj) = (h_R(q,di)-h_R(q,dj))-(s(q,di)-s(q,dj))`. Penalizing this quantity is pairwise margin distillation. A covariance vocabulary does not make that algebra a new objective. Different pair weighting could still be empirically useful, but no new weighting theorem, optimization guarantee, or advantage over the equally weighted/weighted baseline was established. No empirical win is assumed.

**Falsifiable originality statement and outcome.** The tested intellectual claim was: “An orthogonal calibration trained to reduce pairwise low-precision score-error differences supplies a capability beyond conventional rank-aware rotation/distillation.” Its required differentiator would be a new error-control property, strictly broader supported setting, or new optimization/execution rule that survives comparison with the formula above. The proposed rule is expressible by that same formula without adding a component. The claimed distinction therefore fails constructively, before benchmarking. This is stronger evidence than merely finding similarly named papers.

One clearly different family was considered: reciprocal query/document diagonal scaling, especially powers of two intended to avoid additional representational error. Exact dot-product invariance is established; for normal-range binary rounding, reciprocal dyadic scaling leaves each quantized product unchanged. The [mathematical report](PHASE11_MATHEMATICAL_FOUNDATION.md) proves the restricted no-change property and records a CPU check. Arbitrary non-dyadic scaling returns to conventional quantization-aware preprocessing. No new guarantee or special NPU execution rule followed. This alternative does not justify extending the search into another benchmark campaign.

**Answers to the ten required questions.**

1. Similar objectives and transformations exist. No identical FP16 consumer-NPU implementation was verified, and none is claimed.
2. Learned orthogonal quantization, structured random rotations, retrieval-aware distortion, and fixed-teacher ranking distillation are established.
3. The closest equivalent *baseline* is a rotation-constrained, frozen-encoder ranking-distillation student, combining the applicable established mechanisms. No single cited paper is misrepresented as implementing this entire baseline on our NPU.
4. Its unresolved limitation is the lack of a justified model of the installed scorer's arithmetic and of guaranteed benefit on unseen queries. The investigated candidate does not solve either limitation.
5. No sufficiently differentiated mechanism was identified. A measured hardware error model with a new optimization property could be research, but neither “hardware-aware” nor a device loss oracle supplies it automatically.
6. The actual candidate is formalized above and reduces to the baseline. Standard error decomposition and margin conditions do not supply missing originality.
7. The difference currently consists of quantizer, task and platform choices. No new scientific interaction was established.
8. The NPU is essential to evaluating numerical relevance, but not essential to the candidate algorithm's definition; the same objective can wrap another scorer.
9. No advantage over a straightforward rank-aware rotation has been demonstrated. The candidate is that comparator.
10. There is insufficient originality evidence to justify substantial implementation or a confirmatory experiment. The instructed novelty stopping rule applies.

**Implementation-level verification.** Public source was inspected at these pinned revisions:

- `facebookresearch/SpinQuant`, `8f47aa3f00e8662caf1a484153920a07e5281c3a`: `optimize_rotation.py` freezes base parameters and constructs SGDG with `stiefel=True`; `train_utils/optimizer.py` implements Cayley updates.
- `spcl/QuaRot`, `5008669b08c1f11f9b64d52d16fddd47ca754c5a`: `fake_quant/rotation_utils.py` supplies random/structured rotations and applies corresponding query/key transforms.
- `staoxiao/LibVQ`, `ad65c28b75a7434ea073a9750d01142added6d5f`: `LibVQ/models/learnable_vq.py` implements softmax teacher-to-student cross-entropy. Its inspected code does not establish that all the paper's alternative ranking losses are present in that file; the margin/RankNet comparison comes from the paper.

External training tools were not installed or executed. The full ACL PDF timed out through the research tool; its official abstract/bibliography support only the HPS/TRM summary used here. ScienceDirect full-text requests returned 403; R11 is limited to searchable publisher snippets. The decision does not depend on either inaccessible implementation. All numerical-performance claims from other papers remain their authors' results, not CATS-NPU observations.

**Disposition.** Gate A fails for the concrete candidates examined. The new hardware-damage question remains open and is not inferred from historical selection or FP16 conversion experiments. The [final decision](PHASE11_FINAL_RESEARCH_DECISION.md) closes this investigation without claiming a calibration method or measured retrieval benefit.
